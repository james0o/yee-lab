How the metrics are calculated

At every point x of the diagram, let dmin(x) be the distance to the nearest candidate, da(x) the distance to the candidate the method elects, r(x) = da(x) − dmin(x), and L the median distance between two candidates. The loss is ℓ(x) = r(x)/(r(x)+L): 0 where the nearest candidate wins, near 1 where a far one does. Points where no candidate wins (a Condorcet cycle) are no result and are left out.

The error e is the mean of ℓ over an area, and erandom the same for a candidate drawn at random at every point (the mean of ℓ over the candidates). The table shows √(e / erandom): 0% where the nearest candidate wins everywhere, 100% for no better than chance. It does so twice, with the same formula over two areas: the whole diagram, and the region around the candidates (dashed outline, “metrics region” under “Show on diagram”): the convex hull of the candidates and of the Voronoi vertices near them (where three cells or more meet), with a margin of a quarter of the deviation around it. Far from every candidate all methods agree, and those points dilute the whole-diagram error.

The error is computed once a candidate is dropped or a slider let go, not while dragging (—).

After switching to another method, the last column shows how many times smaller (green, ↓) or larger (red, ↑) the error e is than with the method shown before, for the same candidates and voters.
